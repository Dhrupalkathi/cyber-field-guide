# YouTube Channels & Video Learning

Videos can make difficult topics easier to visualize, but they can also become outdated quickly. Use videos to support a structured course or official documentation—not as the only source for commands, product behavior, or security decisions.

## Networking and general IT

### Bitten Tech

- **Best for:** Cybersecurity-oriented networking explanations. The channel has a series titled "Networking for Hackers".
- **Suggested level:** Beginner to intermediate, depending on the video.
- **How to use it:** Pair the videos with Cisco Networking Academy's Networking Basics course. Draw diagrams and verify protocol details using reliable references.
- **Channel:** [Bitten Tech](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC3PsooDxvFG0aEBe4JVtAbg)
- **Review state:** Channel identity and series existence checked on 31 October 2026; no individual video is project-certified.

### NetworkChuck

- **Best for:** Broad IT introductions, networking, Linux, virtual machines, cloud topics, and certification-oriented explanations.
- **Suggested level:** Beginner to intermediate.
- **How to use it:** Good for orientation and motivation. Verify installation instructions and commands against official documentation before applying them.
- **Channel:** [NetworkChuck](https://www.youtube.com/@NetworkChuck)
- **Review state:** Channel page checked on 31 October 2026; individual videos are not project-certified.

## How the club will review video recommendations

A useful video listing should identify:
- the exact channel and video or playlist;
- the topic and intended level;
- whether it is a complete course or a short supplement;
- the publication date when version-sensitive;
- whether commands or product screens may now be outdated;
- the official documentation a learner should cross-check.

## Video safety and quality checklist

Do not blindly copy terminal commands from a video. Pause and inspect what a command does, where it connects, what files it changes, and whether the activity is within an authorized lab. Be cautious with affiliate links, unofficial downloads, "one-click" scripts, and claims that a tool guarantees security or a job.

Contributors should recommend specific videos or playlists—not simply add a channel because it is popular.
