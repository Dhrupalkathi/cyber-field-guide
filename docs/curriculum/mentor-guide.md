# Stage 0/1 — Mentor Guidance and Content Quality

> **Status: Working curriculum draft v1.0 · Last resource review: 31 October 2026.**  
> This guide supports the working CINS Stage 0/Stage 1 curriculum. It is not a claim of formal DAU/CINS approval.

## Mentor approach

- Explain concepts in simple language before introducing technical terminology.
- Use diagrams, analogies, and clear demonstrations when they help a learner understand.
- Encourage students to ask questions and explore topics that interest them.
- Respect different starting points: some students may already know programming or computer fundamentals.
- Offer optional deeper reading to experienced learners without making it compulsory for everyone.
- Keep Stage 0 and Stage 1 accessible on an ordinary laptop or desktop with a web browser.

## Supporting AI-guided learning

Learners may use an AI assistant as a personal tutor to explore any topic in the stage they are studying. Mentors can help them write better prompts, identify what they do not understand, and find reliable sources to verify AI responses.

Mentors should reinforce these rules:

- AI output can be incomplete or incorrect. Important claims must be checked against official documentation or other reliable sources.
- Learners should be able to explain material in their own words before submitting it.
- Never paste credentials, private keys, personal records, confidential club files, unpublished vulnerabilities, or sensitive logs into public AI tools.
- Do not follow AI-generated commands or instructions that the learner does not understand.
- All technical examples must stay within the current stage and its safety boundaries.

## Learner-created material and publication

Learners are encouraged to turn topics they understand into original notes, diagrams, explanations, glossaries, and resource pages.

Before material is published in the CINS repository, a reviewer should check:

- Technical accuracy against reliable sources.
- Clear explanations suitable for the stated learner level.
- Safe and authorized examples.
- Privacy and removal of credentials or confidential information.
- Source attribution, copyright, and disclosure of significant AI assistance.

AI-generated text must not be copied directly into the public guide without the learner understanding it and a human reviewer checking it. Contributions should follow the [club contribution guide](../club/contributing.md).

## Accessibility and equipment

- Stage 0 and Stage 1 should work on an ordinary laptop or desktop with a web browser.
- Do not require Kali Linux, a paid licence, a high-end GPU, or a local virtualisation setup.
- Offer a browser-based option for Python where possible.
- If a student has limited hardware or internet access, mentors should provide downloadable notes and small sample files where practical.
- A student should not be excluded from the foundations because they cannot install a specialised lab.

## Resource maintenance and review

Review the curriculum at least once per academic year and whenever a platform changes a course, retires a path, or changes access conditions.

For every resource, record:

- Resource name and official URL.
- Topic/module it supports.
- Beginner/intermediate/advanced level.
- Whether the content is free, freemium, or paid (verify at review time).
- Whether an account or special hardware is needed.
- Last link-check date.
- Last content-review date.
- Review status: Draft, Link checked, Content reviewed, Lab verified, Needs review, or Archived.

A reachable link does not mean a course has been completed or its teaching quality has been verified. Keep link checks, content reviews, and completed course records distinct.
